1. Project Overview & Problem Statement
Highereducationlaboratoriesinengineering, physicalsciences, andtechnologyfacethreesystemicoperationalchallenges:
1.High Student-to-Instructor Ratio:A single instructor typically supervises 30 to 40 students simultaneously
across 15 to 20 workbench stations, creating instructional bottlenecks and delayed student feedback during practical
lab procedures.
2.Laboratory Safety & Equipment Hazards:Introductory students frequently risk personal injury or hardware
destruction due to incorrect wiring (short circuits, reverse polarity), improper chemical reagent handling, or exceeding
rated power limits.
3.Prohibitive T echnology Adoption Costs:Existing commercial intelligent laboratory robotics require dedicated
high-performance edge computing units (e.g., NVIDIA Jetson Orin at $500+ per bench), making campus-wide lab
deployments financially infeasible.
The IQO Bot Solution:An adaptive multimodal assistant designed to provide real-time, non-invasive bench guidance.
To overcome cost barriers, the AI intelligence is centralized on the existing university server infrastructure, while each
student workbench hosts an ultra-low-cost client node ($63 BOM) with stationary non-locomotive kinematics.
2. Core Architectural Paradigm: Central University Server Hosting
Rather than distributing costly edge GPUs across individual student desks, IQO Bot decouples perception and actuation
from neural reasoning:
•Central School Server (Inference Core):Hosted on existing campus server or workstation hardware. Utilizes
open-source quantized 4-bit Small Language Models (e.g., LLaMA-3 8B, Qwen-2.5 7B) served via Ollama or vLLM
over the university local area network (LAN). This completely eliminates per-token cloud API fees ($0 ongoing
operating cost) and ensures 100% student privacy and zero data leakage.
•Lightweight W orkbench Client Nodes:Compact, affordable microcomputing units (Raspberry Pi 4 / ESP32-S3)
interfacing with a standard USB webcam, microphone array, and 3-DOF articulated pointer arm.
•Deterministic Safety Guardrails:Critical laboratory safety rules (e.g., electrical overcurrent thresholds, banned
chemical mixtures) are enforced via local deterministic regex/rule filters prior to LLM generation, ensuring zero
hallucination on safety-critical directives.

3.7. Dual F orm F actor Delivery: Physical Robot vs. 3D W eb Simulator
•Physical Benchtop Robot:Stationary C-clamp chassis mounted directly to workbench frame (eliminating wheeled
locomotion to avoid tipping over sensitive glassware or high-voltage lines). Features a 2-DOF pan-tilt head with
expressive LED eye display and a 3-DOF articulated pointer arm with an optical focus dot for indicating components.
•3D Digital T win W eb Simulator:DevelopedinThree.js/WebGL,mirroringthephysicalrobot’sinversekinematics
and laboratory environment in the browser. Allows distance learners and students preparing for practical sessions to
run dry-run experiments using identical REST/WebSocket server APIs.
3.8. Conclusion & Educational Roadmap
IQO Bot demonstrates that modern embodied AI laboratory assistance does not require prohibitive per-bench capital
investment. By centralizing neural intelligence on standard school servers and deploying $71 client nodes, academic
institutions can scale personalized, real-time safety supervision and pedagogical guidance across entire laboratory co-
horts. Future milestones include expanding real-time optical breadboard trace validation and multi-student cooperative
conversational tracking.
